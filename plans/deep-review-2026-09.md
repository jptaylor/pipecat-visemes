## 1. Executive summary

The implementation is sound engineering: the processor's timing model, the pass-through safety, the P² estimators, Levinson, slot assignment and the event state machines all check out (several small bugs listed in §7, none catastrophic). Accuracy is limited by five things, in this order:

1. **LPC order 12 at 16 kHz is pole-starved.** On any signal with realistic energy above ~3.5 kHz the order-12 fit spends pole pairs above 4 kHz, F1/F2 merge into broad roots, the 500 Hz bandwidth cap empties the slots, and the analyzer runs on held values and the `f1_broad` workaround. On the real clip the strict F1 slot is filled on **0 %** of Praat-voiced hops at order 12 and **71 %** at order 16 (f1_mae 10 Hz at ceiling 5000); on the espeak corpus order 14/16 cut f1_mae 78 → 27/48 Hz and lift vowel openness correlation 0.36 → 0.63/0.71 for +8 % CPU. This is the single largest lever. *Must validate on real fixtures*, but the mechanism is textbook (one pole pair per kHz; Praat uses order 10 at 10 kHz).
2. **The signal is late by design, and the client makes it worse.** Causal median-3 + 0.25/hop slew add 1–2 hops of lag (measured +32…+53 ms to 50 % crossing; +40…+80 ms best cross-correlation shift on speech-like signals), and `_emit_batches` holds every batch until the analyzer is 400 ms past the window end. A pacing simulation shows the first batch of every utterance is emitted **after** its audio starts playing unless TTS streams ≥ ~4× real time (−89 ms at 10×, −345 ms at 2×, −640 ms at 1×); a mid-turn LLM stall holds the last ~0.6 s of keyframes until the next sentence arrives (0.9–1.3 s late). The client then anchors the whole utterance on that late arrival plus an assumed 200 ms lead: simulated anchor error **+284 ms (10×) to +836 ms (1×)** at utterance start, staircasing forward as later batches arrive, and settling 70–90 ms early because it anchors on the first keyframe rather than the window start; barge-in leaves ~400 ms of stale motion. Zero-phase conditioning (offset −20 ms), emitting keyframes 1–2 hops after the window end, an idle flush, and two extra floats on the wire (`ws`, `lead`) remove essentially all of it at zero CPU.
3. **Consonants are invisible.** Closure events are only a badge on the client; the keyframe track decays 20 %/hop, so during a p/b/m the mouth sits at openness 0.44 (target 0). An energy gate on the openness target (mouth closes with the energy dip, no confirmation needed) takes that to 0.11 and lifts openness correlation over all speech from 0.31 to 0.46 on the espeak corpus. Unvoiced fricatives inherit the previous vowel's shape although the already-computed centroid / high-band ratio separate /s/ /ʃ/ /f/ cleanly.
4. **Adaptation and confidence misfire.** The distribution-shift detector fires on ordinary vowel sequences (twice in 6.6 s of one voice), resetting convergence and dragging ranges back to priors; confidence is mostly a loudness meter and the client uses it as opacity rather than the documented lerp-to-neutral; the 1-hop nasal fast path latches on /i/→/u/ transitions.
5. **The benchmark cannot see 1–4.** 30 of the composite's 100 points are structurally free (count-range event checks that pass with 0.00 closure precision; a convergence metric that scores 100 on any clip under 3 s); L1 scores only frames where we committed a slot (coverage failures are invisible, coverage gains raise MAE — exactly the trap recorded in `accuracy-improvements-2.md`); L2 is Pearson r at zero lag over Praat-voiced frames, so it masks out all consonants, bakes in the 1-D F1/F2 mapping, and is blind to the 40–80 ms lag and to gain/jitter; timing and the client are not measured at all; the Praat reference flips by ~200 Hz F1 between ceilings 5000 and 5500 on the same clip; and fixtures and baseline are gitignored, so a fresh clone cannot reproduce 70.9. Roughly, 70.9 ≈ 29/40 (L1) + 12/30 (L2) + 30/30 (free).

**Top 10 by expected perceived-quality gain per unit effort** (the completeness pass's ranking across all 21 reviews, tags refer to sections below): 1 [CLIENT-1]+[WIRE-2] `ws` and `lead` on the wire, client anchors on them; 2 [LAT-1]+[LAT-2] per-kind horizon split, idle flush, restart keyed on `ws`, with [PROC-1]; 3 [BENCH-1..4, 6] composite v1.5 and reproducible fixtures; 4 [DSP-1]+[DSP-2]+[DSP-3] order 16, NCC voicing, slot prior, `c_fit` and nasal re-tune as one bundle; 5 [ADAPT-1] shift detector; 6 [CONS-1] closures as a visible aperture dip; 7 [COND-1] one-hop re-stamp, slew 0.4, anchor keyframe; 8 [CONF-1]+[CLIENT-4] confidence recalibration and no opacity coupling; 9 [CONS-2]+[ROUND-1] sibilant class and rounding gate; 10 text-gated closures with the L4 layer to measure them (§8 stage D step 1).

The roadmap (§8) therefore starts with measurement (coverage, timing, a phoneme-level layer), then the two DSP changes (order, voicing), then conditioning/latency, then consonants, then the packaging work that makes it a drop-in. The structural ceiling of a formant tracker is consonant identity; the cheapest way past it that stays CPU-bound is **text-informed alignment** — the pipeline already delivers each sentence's text before its audio and, for 12 providers, playout-timed word frames — and a prototype DTW on the proxy lifted closure precision/recall from 0.15/0.41 to 0.81/0.80 with word anchors; a tiny learned regressor trained offline on forced-aligned TTS audio is the complementary step. Both keep numpy-only inference.

## 2. DSP (`server/lipsync/dsp.py`)

### 2.1 Verified correct
`levinson_durbin`, `lpc_coefficients`, `_assign_slots`, `rms_energy`, `spectral_nasal_features` and `P2QuantileEstimator` match their reference formulas; P² tracks `np.percentile` within ~1 % on lognormal, uniform, quantized, ramp, constant, bimodal and step streams; float32 vs float64 LPC differ by ≤ 0.02 Hz at order 16. Window shape (Hamming/Hann/Gaussian), window length (15–50 ms), hop, and autocorrelation vs covariance vs Burg were all measured and are **not** levers (± 3 Hz at equal order). High-F0 harmonic bias (15–45 Hz at f0 250–320 Hz) is shared by Praat itself, so it cannot move the benchmark.

### 2.2 [DSP-1] LPC order — the dominant lever (P1, S effort, *must validate on real fixtures*)
- Evidence: `dsp.py:37` `LPC_ORDER = 12` at 16 kHz; `dsp.py:42,300` discards roots with bandwidth > 500 Hz. KlattGrid /a/ (700/1200/2500) at order 12 → roots (815 Hz, bw 260), (1275, bw 822), (2807, 624)…: F2 discarded, F1 +115 Hz; order 14 → (705, 78), (1211, 124); order 16 → (698, 43), (1200, 66). Real clip (`ready.wav`, Praat ceiling 5000):

  | 14 | 0.07 | 0.86 | 72 (n=1) | 187 |
  | 16 | 0.71 | 0.93 | 10 | 104 |
  | 18 | 0.71 | 1.00 | 12 | 151 |

  Espeak corpus (68 clips, Praat reference + phoneme targets): order 12 → 14 → 16 → 18: f1_mae 78 → 27 → 48 → 66; vowel openness r vs phoneme targets 0.36 → 0.63 → 0.71 → 0.73; f2_mae 184 → 175 → 180 → 174; order 20 regresses f2. An independent run through the **real harness** (`analyze_clip`/`compute_reference`/`compute_metrics`, 12 corpus sentences × 4 espeak voices):

  | 14 | 0.76 | 0.85 | 20.4 | 96 | 0.51 | 0.50 | 0.77 | 73.9 |

  Note f1_mae *rising* from 20 to 36 Hz as coverage rises from 0.76 to 0.98 — the masking artefact of §6.2 — and voicing agreement falling with order because the residual pitch detector whitens with the same LPC fit ([DSP-2]). Clean three-resonator synthetic vowels show no difference between 12 and 16 (which is why the unit tests never caught this — `tests/synth.py:24` has no energy above F3); with F4–F8 added (bandwidths 200–400 Hz, as in real 16 kHz speech) the same configuration reads F1/F2 MAE **219/253 Hz with a +193 Hz F1 bias**, order 14 still 185/194, order 16 **18/17**, order 18 19/17, 10 kHz/order 10 17.5/15.7, 11 kHz/order 12 18/17 — the under-modelling is unambiguous on exact truth. Side effect to manage: the nasal detector is coupled to the order through `f2_damped` (`:443–447`), which treats a missing or broad F2 as nasal evidence; at order 12 F2 is absent on 22 % of voiced proxy frames, at order 16 it is found on 95 % with narrower bandwidths and NASAL events on the same clips drop 14 → 0, so re-tune `_NASAL_F2_MAX_BANDWIDTH_HZ` / `_NASAL_SPURIOUS_F2_HZ` and the `f1_broad` admission in the same change and keep nasal-hum 4/4 green on the real fixtures. A second lever surfaced by the same run: the **LPC window length** — 40 ms (`FRAME_SIZE` 640, hop unchanged) at order 16 lifts the proxy composite 74.6 → 78.7 (openness_r 0.56, f2_mae 88); 50 ms over-smooths; decouple the energy/closure framing (keep 25 ms) from the LPC framing so event timing is not smeared (closures per clip dropped 6.0 → 5.2 with a shared frame).
- Proposal: `LPC_ORDER = 16` (test 14 and 18 on the real corpus as well), land together with [DSP-2] (the residual pitch detector breaks at higher order: a 120 Hz glottal train reads 92 / 64 Hz at order 14 / 16), recalibrate `_C_FIT_LOG10_FULL` (see §3.7), and keep the `f1_broad` path (`dsp.py:59,314–322`) until corpus coverage at order 16 is confirmed ≥ 0.9 on open vowels — it was a symptom of merged poles and should then go. Keep the 500 Hz bandwidth cap. Verified independently: pole starvation reproduces on synthetic vowels with extra high resonators, on a from-vowel KlattGrid, and on the real clip (CPU +24 µs/hop); the harness's own f1_mae stays NaN at every order on that clip because fewer than 8 frames commit, which is exactly the blind spot of §6.2. Add the "realistic" test fixture: `synth_vowel(..., extra=[(3500,200),(4500,250),(5500,300),(6500,300)])` with a coverage assertion (both slots filled ≥ 90 % of steady frames), which fails at order 12 and passes at 14/16.
- Alternative: the Praat-matched path — analysis at 10 kHz, order 10 — matches the reference by construction on steady vowels and equals order 16 on the real clip, but through the full harness on the proxy it is **not more accurate** (composite 74.2 vs 74.6 at 25 ms; 80.1 vs 79.8 at 40 ms with the prior), only 20–30 % cheaper (RTF 0.013–0.015 vs 0.019), and its residual pitch detector happens to work better (voicing 0.84). Keep it as the CPU-saving variant, not the accuracy path; it needs pitch lags 25..166 with parabolic interpolation, `_LOW_BAND_BINS`, buffers and the nasal thresholds re-tuned.
- Impact: expected f1_mae well under the 50 Hz full-credit threshold, openness_r/width_r up materially, and the L2/L4 correlation gains above. CPU +30–35 µs/hop.

### 2.3 [DSP-2] Voicing: replace residual autocorrelation with a normalized cross-correlation on the raw frame (P1, S)
- Evidence: `dsp.py:326–360`: inverse-filtered residual → FFT autocorrelation → lag gain up to 10× at long lags (`_PITCH_LAG_GAIN`, `dsp.py:80–87`) → smallest lag ≥ 0.85 × peak → clarity > 0.35. On KlattGrid vowels with aspiration noise: agreement with Praat voicing 0.97 at 40 dB SNR, 0.72–0.97 at 15 dB with 23–38 % pitch-halving for f0 ≥ 180, 0.08–0.36 at 5 dB; and the estimator is coupled to LPC order (unit test reads 92/64 Hz for a 120 Hz train at order 14/16). An NCC on the DC-removed raw frame (sliding-window matmul, same cost: 41 vs 39 µs) holds 0.97 at every f0/SNR tested and 0.94 vs Praat on the real clip with a 3 %-of-peak energy gate. Corpus voicing agreement today is 0.67 (plans) / 0.81 (espeak).
- Proposal: `ncc_pitch(raw_frame)`: lags 40..266, `np.correlate(x[lag_min:], x[:seg], 'valid')` normalized by sliding energies from one cumsum (≈ 20 µs, cheaper than the residual path; the einsum/`sliding_window_view` form costs 57–68 µs), the local maximum inside the first contiguous run ≥ 0.85 × peak (not its first sample), parabolic interpolation, voiced = peak > ~0.6–0.7 AND rms > 0.03 × recent peak (the gate is essential: ungated NCC over-voices the real clip, 0.56–0.61 vs Praat 0.39). Pass the raw frame (the analyzer passes `self._windowed` at `formant_lipsync_analyzer.py:419`; NCC on the pre-emphasized frame collapses to 0.03 agreement at 5 dB). Tune the threshold on the corpus; keep the residual path as a fallback flag for one release.
- Impact: primarily the **enabler for [DSP-1]** — residual voicing recall falls 0.76 → 0.69 on the proxy as order goes 12 → 16 because the detector whitens with the same fit, so landing order 16 alone trades ~7 points of voicing recall — plus noise robustness (0 octave errors at every f0/SNR tested, 0.98 agreement down to 15 dB; real clip 0.81 → 0.92 with the gate). Expect voicing_agreement toward ~0.85 on real fixtures; every recovered voiced frame stops decaying the mouth to neutral. CPU neutral to −15 µs/hop with the `np.correlate` form.

### 2.4 [DSP-3] Slot assignment lock-in — a one-line fix worth ~40 Hz of F2 (P1, S; land with [DSP-1])
- Evidence: `_assign_slots` (`dsp.py:214–258`) minimizes deviation from the *previous frame's* slots and falls back to the nominal centres only when a slot is empty, so once F2 lands on the F3 track it never returns even though narrow true-F2 roots are present every frame (e.g. m3 voice t = 1.71 s: (2301, 2954) beats (911, 2301) under prev-relative deviation). Direct attribution on the proxy at order 16: F2 is assigned to the wrong root *while the correct root is among the candidates* on 11 % of frames (F1: 2 %); swap-like jumps are rare (1 of 1968 frames), so jump statistics cannot see it.
- Proposal: add an absolute prior to the greedy deviation — `deviation += |f − nominal|/nominal` per filled slot, λ ≈ 1.0, optionally `+ 0.6·bw/500` — so continuity can never lock a slot onto the wrong track indefinitely; shift the nominals with the pitch-probe prior like the F1/F2 ranges. Measured: f2_mae 101 → 62 Hz, width_r 0.554 → 0.594, composite 74.6 → 77.3 at zero CPU (λ 0.25/0.5/1.0: 86/73/62 Hz; bandwidth penalty alone 90; log-frequency deviation is worse, 117). Expose candidate roots in the debug tap and add the "wrong-with-candidate" attribution and a slot-swap counter to the harness. A fixed-lag Kalman/Viterbi smoother (KARMA/ESPS-style) is **not** worth building: the optimal full-utterance Viterbi over the order-12 candidates — the upper bound for any fixed-lag version — gains only 3.6/6 Hz (f1_mae 73.7 → 70.1, f2_mae 186.6 → 180.6) versus −50 Hz from the order change. `_assign_slots` costs 4–15 µs on real frames; not worth vectorizing.

### 2.5 [PERF-1] CPU (P4, S/M)
Not a problem today (≈ 260–440 µs per hop depending on measurement, 1.3–2 % of a core), but free wins if many bots share a host, all with bit-identical or numerically identical output: (a) replace `np.median` on 3-element lists (`formant_lipsync_analyzer.py:797` and the shift ring at 620) with `sorted(hist)[1]` — measured −30…−37 % analyzer CPU; (b) replace the Python Levinson loop with `np.linalg.solve` on the order×order Toeplitz matrix built from the same autocorrelation (coefficients within 1e-6, −22 µs/hop; keep `prediction_gain = r[0]/(r[0] + a[1:]·r[1:])`); (c) `np.linalg.eigvals` on the companion matrix directly instead of `np.roots` (−10 µs); (d) later, batch every drained chunk (TTS bursts deliver many hops at once) with `sliding_window_view` framing and stacked eigvals. Keep `test_analyzer_chunk_invariance`.

## 3. Signal model (`server/lipsync/formant_lipsync_analyzer.py`)

### 3.1 [COND-1] Zero-phase conditioning and anticipatory offsets (P1, S)
- Evidence: `_condition` (`:791–808`) is a trailing 3-tap median then a 0.25/hop slew; keyframes are stamped at the hop center (`:382`). Synthetic /a/→/i/→/u/→/a/ with 50 ms ramps: raw mapped openness crosses 50 % at +13 ms after the ramp center, the emitted track at +32 ms (A→I, I→U) and +53 ms for the full-range U→A step (slew needs 4 hops). Random 45-vowel sequence scored like the harness: raw open_r/width_r 0.958/0.948, current conditioning 0.866/0.900, centered median-3 0.951/0.940. Espeak corpus: cross-correlation lag +1.16 hops; removing the median → L2 open_r +0.07, width_r +0.07, lag 0.43 hops, but keyframe rate +3/s; keeping median-3 and **shifting emitted offsets −20 ms** gives the same L2 gain with no rate change; −40 ms gives L2 open_r +0.11, width_r +0.08 and lag −0.15 hops (slightly early); −60 ms overshoots.
- Proposal: make the median zero-phase by **buffering one hop and emitting hop t−1 after seeing hop t** (verified: the "subtract `HOP_SECONDS` from the offset" shortcut gives the first keyframe a negative offset and desynchronizes keyframes from events, which are stamped from unshifted hop times at `:717, :737`); raise slew to 0.4/hop (verified twice on 45-vowel sequences: 0.701/0.668 → 0.747/0.750 and 0.893/0.828 → 0.967/0.925 openness/width r, still better than no median under 8 % spike frames, emitted lag on opening steps 27 → 7 ms, keyframe rate 25.1 → 21.1/s — which also clears the marginal 25.4/s guard in `accuracy-improvements-2.md`). The minimal form (`offset − HOP_SECONDS` clamped ≥ 0) is acceptable if events are re-stamped consistently. Add an optional `anticipation_ms` parameter **defaulting to 0**: the literature supports a visual lead (ITU-R BT.1359 detectability ~+45 ms audio-lead vs −125 ms video-lead; mouths lead the acoustics by 20–70 ms in CV syllables), but a blanket pre-shift was refuted on the proxy: after the structural fixes the closure event leads the true onset by a median 22 ms and the openness minimum trails it by only +3 ms (first keyframe vs first phone +1 ms), so set the client default trim to about −20 ms and let the L5 signed lead/lag histogram decide anything further. Also emit an **anchor keyframe**: while a parameter sits inside the 0.05 dead band no keyframe is emitted for up to 240 ms, so when the next transition crosses the band the client (and the harness) interpolate from a keyframe up to 240 ms old and the mouth starts moving *before* the acoustics; emit the previous hop's value first when a band crossing is detected (measured +0.04/+0.05 r on top of the lag fix). Test: /u/→/a/ step reaches 50 % within one hop of the raw crossing.
- Impact: openness_r/width_r +0.05–0.10 each on proxies (≈ +1.5–3 composite), visible tightening of sync; zero CPU.

### 3.2 [CONS-1] Close the mouth on energy dips, server side (P1, S)
- Evidence: any unvoiced hop decays each parameter 20 %/hop toward 0.15/0.35 (`:484–487`); vowel + 80 ms gap + vowel emits openness 0.62, 0.53, 0.45, 0.39 across the gap although a CLOSURE event (conf 1.0) is emitted; the client renders events only as a badge (`Mouth.tsx:88–95`, `WireframeMouth.tsx:266–270`). Espeak corpus: openness during p/b/m 0.44; closure-event precision 0.15, recall 0.41 (416 events for 176 true bilabials; 192 land on t/d/k/g — the detector is a stop/pause detector, `types.py:24` documents it as bilabial). Prototype gate `openness_target *= smoothstep(rms, 1.0·thr, 2.5·thr)` with `thr = max(3·noise_floor, 0.22·recent_peak)`: bilabial openness 0.44 → 0.11, nasal segments closed 47 % → 73 %, openness r over all speech 0.31 → 0.46, lag −0.6 hops; vowel-only r −0.05 (edges of vowels close earlier). A tighter gate (1.0–1.6·thr) gives 0.19 / 0.58 / 0.42.
- Proposal: during a low run inside speech set the openness target toward ~0.05 immediately (the slew closes it in 2–3 hops; verified prototype: 0.75, 0.50, 0.25, 0.05 within 60 ms) — or the smoothstep gate above — set `_SILENCE_REST_OPENNESS` to the same 0.05 so a pause does not re-open 0.05 → 0.15, and keep the event for lip-press styling only, using its raw `duration` (the client's `activeEvents` floors spans at 250 ms, `feed.ts:29, :225`, so a 40 ms closure must not pin the mouth shut for 250 ms). The detector is a level test (`closure_thresh = max(3·noise_floor, 0.22·recent_peak)`, `:706–709`): a 120 ms noise burst at 8 % of vowel RMS also fires CLOSURE, and the event's `confidence` is just the dip depth (`:733`) — alveolar/velar dips score p50 1.00 vs 0.96 for real bilabials — so **no confidence threshold anywhere in the stack can filter false closures**; only place information (text, §8) can. Rendering every dip as a brief *openness* dip (jaw closes for every stop) is therefore the right default, with lip-seal styling reserved for closures that carry a bilabial label. Keep trailing-speech confirmation for the event: emitting closures at dip onset would add ~31 % more events of which only 19 % land on real bilabials. Retune the gate on real fixtures against the vowel-probe checks.
- Impact: every stop and short pause becomes a visible closure; the largest single perceptual gain on "Bob put a big pepper" speech; zero CPU. Requires the closure check of §6.6a to be scored.

### 3.3 [CONS-2] Unvoiced fricative shapes (P2, M)
- Evidence: /a/ + 150 ms sibilant + /i/: keyframes through the /s/ still read the /a/ pose (openness 0.62→0.55, width 0.26→0.31) at confidence 0.02–0.07. On the analyzer's own pre-emphasized 512-pt spectrum: /s/ centroid 6.3 kHz, > 4 kHz ratio 1.00; /ʃ/ 3.5 kHz, 0.36; /h/ 2.2 kHz; /f/ and /θ/ indistinguishable from each other (as phonetics predicts) but distinct from /s/ by level.
- Proposal: an unvoiced branch when `rms ≥ silence_gate and not voiced`: sibilant-spread (high centroid, hi-ratio > 0.7) → (openness 0.12, width 0.7, rounding 0); postalveolar (mid centroid) → (0.15, 0.3, 0.6); weak diffuse → (0.1, 0.4, 0); require 2 consecutive hops; keep confidence low. On the espeak proxy the unvoiced centroid separates /s z/ from /ʃ tʃ/ with **AUC 0.93** — the one "free" spectral cue with strong class separation — while /f v/ sit *above* /ʃ/ in centroid (AUC 0.29) and must be told apart by relative level (rms vs recent peak), not spectrum. All thresholds must be calibrated on the pre-emphasized spectrum the analyzer actually computes (`dsp.py:379–396`; +6 dB/octave tilt moves the quoted Hz values) and on real fixtures. Shaped noise also false-voices the residual detector — 12 % of /s/ hops, 17 % of /h/, and **6 of 8 /ʃ/ hops, which then map garbage roots to a near-fully-open mouth (0.85–0.92)** — so land in two steps: (1) S — a voicing guard (voiced requires clarity > 0.5 or hi-ratio < 0.3; drops 0 % of vowel hops) plus the sibilant-spread class only, with 2-hop hysteresis, guarded by f1/f2 MAE and keyframe rate; (2) M — weak-diffuse (level-first precedence, rms < 0.3 × peak) and postalveolar classes once the sibilant probe of §6 exists. Pair with [DSP-2] and evaluate against `voicing_agreement` and the nasal detector (which requires voiced). Labiodental /f v/ cannot be recovered acoustically — the first concrete use for text (§8, text-informed tier).
- Impact: ~10 % of English phones by count; not measurable until §6 adds a consonant layer.

### 3.4 [ADAPT-1] Distribution-shift detector fires on ordinary speech (P1 bug, S)
- Evidence: `:618–626`: once the 100-frame ring is full, if median(last 25 voiced F1) deviates > 25 % from the ring median, `_prior_decay = 1.0` and `_voiced_frames = 30`. The ring fills after ~2.3–2.6 s of voiced speech and its very first evaluation compares the last 25 frames against all 100, which random vowel content routinely exceeds: 9/9 synthetic runs fired at 2.30–2.50 s (or 0.3–1.2 s into a `--warm` probe) — i.e. inside the benchmark's post-convergence window (t ≥ 1.5 s) and the typical 3 s clip, which is the likely mechanism behind the plans' "accepted residual" vowel-probe failures and why `--warm` runs never stay converged. One synthetic voice speaking a random 45-vowel sequence (6.6 s) fired it twice (hops 147, 256); c_conv went 0.43, 0.85, 1.0, 1.0, 0.53 over 0.5–3 s; effective f1_hi walked 704 → 895 (prior) → 703 → 869 → 758; the harness's `convergence_s` reads "never settled" (6.13 s). The same mechanism explains the `--warm` run ending at voiced_frames 35.
- Proposal (two independent verifications): require **pitch corroboration** — |median F0 of the last 25 voiced frames − ring median F0| / ring median > 15 % — as a necessary condition (0 false fires on five single-voice clips, both F0-changing switches caught); a both-edge P10/P90 range test is useful only *in conjunction* with it (alone it still false-fires on 2/5 clips and misses ×1.12–1.18 formant shifts). The response is also discontinuous: on a fire `_prior_decay = 1.0` makes `_effective_range` (`:661–664`) return the raw prior in the same hop (learned [298, 691] → [250, 899] instantly; a voiced hop at F1 600 Hz jumps from openness 0.77 to 0.54 with only the 0.25 slew downstream) — ramp `_prior_decay` up over ~5 hops, decay toward the previous learned range rather than the priors, and never lower `_voiced_frames` below `_CONVERGENCE_FRAMES` once converged. Record the deviation from spec §4.3 (which specifies the median-25 % test). The regression test needs ≥ 150 voiced frames of varied content so the ring fills. Prefer explicit voice keying ([ADAPT-2]) and keep the detector as the fallback.
- Impact: steady-state sessions stay converged; removes mid-utterance confidence dips (the client fades the mouth when c_conv halves); convergence_s component (weight 5) recovers on long clips.

### 3.5 [ADAPT-2] Key adaptive state by voice id (P2, M)
- Evidence: prior shift happens once per session from the first 10 voiced frames (`:604–612`); a session that starts on a 100 Hz voice and switches to 230 Hz keeps priors (250, 900). Cold sustained /a/ maps to openness p90 0.70 vs 1.00 warm; a 0.6 s utterance ends at c_conv 0.48. `TTSService` swallows `TTSUpdateSettingsFrame`, but `LipsyncUpdateSettingsFrame` already reaches the processor.
- Proposal, by value/effort (the benefit is bounded: state persists across utterances, so cold compression costs ~1.2 s of voiced speech *per session*, not per turn — a 1.8 s clip converges fully): (a) S — an optional persisted per-voice cache (`voice_id → P² estimator states, priors, voiced_frames`) loaded in `start()` so every session after the first starts converged; (b) `voice_id` in `LipsyncParams` via `LipsyncUpdateSettingsFrame` (the fork-free path: `TTSService` consumes `TTSUpdateSettingsFrame` unless addressed elsewhere, its settings are private, and `TTSStartedFrame` carries no voice), plus an optional best-effort `voice_key` callable polled at `TTSStartedFrame`; (c) defer a pitch-class prior table until priors are measured on real voices.
- Impact: first-second compression disappears for known voices; convergence_s → ~0; removes the once-per-session prior-shift trap on voice switches.

### 3.6 [ROUND-1] Rounding gate and F3 (P2, S)
- Evidence: `:476–477` gates rounding by `smoothstep(openness, 0.05, 0.15)`; /u/ (F1 299) maps to openness 0.00–0.04 cold — and *after* adaptation the learned P10 F1 floor coincides with /u/'s F1, so openness is exactly 0.00 and rounding 0.00 where the ungated formula gives 1.00 (/o/ at F1 450 passes at 0.86). Meanwhile /a/ (F2 1200, below the prior midpoint 1650) reads rounding 0.53 during the first 1.2 s and 0.18 after adaptation, matching the proxy's OPEN-class rounding of 0.22 against a target of 0: the formula currently produces the highest rounding for the wrong vowels. This gate, not the missing F3 term, is the defect behind the vowel-oo bar. F3 is tracked (`dsp.py:52`, slot 3) and unused. A free F3 cue `clamp((3000 − F3)/800)` on KlattGrid vowels reads /u/ 1.00, /o/ 0.77, /a/ 0.52, /i/ 0.00 with F3 found on 100 % of voiced hops. (On espeak the best Praat-side cue was absolute F2, `(1200 − F2)/400`, r 0.41 vs 0.35 for normalized width; espeak's F3 is static so it says nothing about F3 on real speech.)
- Proposal (narrowed by verification: the proposed lower edge still zeroes /u/ when it maps to openness exactly 0.00 at f0 220, and F3 is found *least* on rounded vowels — /u/ 58 %, /o/ 79 % at realistic bandwidths): replace the lower openness gate with an **evidence gate** — rounding requires an F2 found this hop (or held ≤ 1 hop) and voiced — and keep only the upper gate `1 − smoothstep(openness, 0.6, 0.8)` so open /a/ is cut (/a/ 0.48–0.58 → 0.04). Do **not** zero rounding while `nasal_active`: a synthetic /u/ at f0 100 fires NASAL on every cycle (dark back vowel with merged F1/F2 satisfies `:757–762`; see §3.8), so that rule would kill /u/ on low voices. Treat the F3 term (`0.5 + 0.5·clamp((f3_hi − F3)/(f3_hi − f3_lo))`, prior [2200, 3000] shifted like F1/F2) as an **experiment to validate on real fixtures**, not a design decision: on synthetic vowels it also reads /æ/ 0.75, with the 0.5 floor a typical /a/ F3 of ~2600 still passes 0.75 of the F2 term, and on the espeak proxy F3 carries no rounding signal at all (AUC 0.50 over 634 rounded vs 1783 unrounded hops — expected, since espeak's F3 is static). Give F3 the same bounded hold/decay as F1/F2 (today `prev_f3` is held forever, `:344, 458–459`). Apply the F3 multiplier only when `f3_found` this hop, never on a held F3. Guards: rounding_p90 ≤ 0.2 on vowel-ee and ≤ 0.3 on vowel-aa.

### 3.7 [CONF-1] Confidence is a loudness meter; the client uses it as opacity (P2, S)
- Evidence: `c_snr` (`:546–548`) is measured against a noise floor capped at 5 % of the recent peak (`:395`), so during pause-free speech it tracks loudness: the same /a/ at −12 dB drops confidence 0.46 → 0.36 with zero formant error. With a warm analyzer on proxy speech 88–91 % of voiced hops score < 0.3 and none > 0.8, so the documented client contract is unusable until this is fixed (§5.4). Worse, `c_fit = log10(prediction_gain)/3` (`:124, :544`) is calibrated on the test generator: prediction gain is 2075 on `synth_vowel` (c_fit 1.0), 38 on a KlattGrid /a/ (0.53), but median 2.5–3.1 on real voiced frames (c_fit ≈ 0.15), which alone explains the ~0.17 mean confidence on real speech reported in the plans. `types.py:49–50` and spec §4.4 define confidence as the lerp-toward-neutral weight; `Mouth.tsx:83` / `WireframeMouth.tsx:174` map it to opacity `0.4 + 0.6·confidence`, so quiet syllables and post-shift resets fade the mouth; under the documented contract the signal would be flattened toward schwa.
- Proposal: recalibrate `c_fit` on real frames (full credit around gain ~10, not 10³; on the proxy mean voiced `c_fit` moves 0.41 → 0.53 from order 12 → 16, so re-derive `_C_FIT_LOG10_FULL` — roughly 3.0 → 3.4 — whenever the order changes, and measure the real-speech distribution where median gain is only 2.5–3.1); drop `c_snr` from the product and report it separately (re-run `test_confidence_discriminates_signal_from_noise`); a silence-only noise floor must bootstrap from the current cap for the first ≥ 10 sub-gate hops because the silence gate itself derives from it (`:399`). Then split the wire meaning: `confidence` stays a per-hop evidence value for diagnostics and the benchmark (confidence tracks loudness at r 0.92 today and the documented contract would halve peak openness 0.76 → 0.36), and clients must not modulate opacity or amplitude by it; cold-start blending, if wanted, keys on `c_conv` (or an explicit `converged` flag) for the first ~1 s only.

### 3.8 [NASAL-1] 1-hop nasal fast path latches on /i/→/u/ (P3, S)
`:765` enters after one hop when `low_ratio > 0.9`; a nasal-free /a/→/i/→/u/→/a/ sequence emits NASAL (conf 0.89) exactly at the /i/→/u/ ramp (F2 root broadens during the sweep while the arriving /u/ is very dark), and a steady synthetic /u/ (F1 300, F2 800) at f0 100 fires NASAL on every cycle of a six-vowel sequence (none at f0 220) because a dark back vowel with merged F1/F2 satisfies `:757–762`. The detector is also coupled to the LPC order (see §2.2: a missing/broad F2 is nasal evidence, and order 16 finds F2 on 95 % of frames). Proposal: always require 2 hops, let the pre-latch soft cap carry the one-hop response, add F1 < 400 Hz and pitch continuity to `nasal_now`, re-tune the F2-damping thresholds together with [DSP-1], and add `nasals_max` to vowel-oo in the corpus; watch nasal-hum p90 and 4/4 on real fixtures (the espeak proxy cannot measure nasals, §0).

### 3.9 [EVENT-1] Place-of-articulation hint on closures — an experiment, not a commitment (P3, M)
The confirming hop already has centroid/low-band ratio from the FFT (`:425`); stop bursts are classically separable by gross spectral shape (bilabial diffuse-falling, alveolar diffuse-rising, velar compact mid). On the espeak proxy, however, a burst-centroid place cue reaches only **AUC 0.60** (95 bilabial vs 189 other confirmed dips), so treat it as fixture-gated: compute the burst spectrum on the *un-emphasized* frame (one extra rFFT, ~12 µs) with thresholds calibrated on real fixtures, classify {bilabial, alveolar, velar, pause}, and carry the label on the event. Cheaper, more certain precision gains first: require a voice-bar / low-band signature or a minimum dip depth for CLOSURE, and expose non-bilabial dips as a distinct `stop` event (or just the keyframe dip of [CONS-1]) so the client still articulates them.

### 3.10 Small verified items
(a) `flush` (`:296–305`) drops pending closures, so a sentence-final stop confirmed by the next context's speech is lost; carry pending closures across a flush when the next context starts within ~150 ms (reset on interruption still drops them). (b) F3 never decays (see 3.6). (c) The `speech_before` slice (`:724`) and hop-center offsets (`:382`) are correct; the closure decision latency is gap + 1 hop with +12 ms offset error; nasal latches in 1 hop; silence fires 15 hops after its stamped offset.

## 4. Timing, processor and Pipecat integration (`server/lipsync/lipsync_processor.py`)

### 4.1 [LAT-1] The 400 ms finalize horizon makes the first batch of every utterance late (P1, M)
- Evidence: `_emit_batches` (`:522`) refuses to emit a batch until `cursor ≥ window_end + EVENT_FINALIZE_HORIZON_SEC` (0.4 s, `formant_lipsync_analyzer.py:33`), so the batch covering [0, 0.2) needs 0.6 s of audio analyzed. `pts = t0 + window_start − lead` is then clamped to `now` (`:600–604`). Headless pipeline simulation (20 ms TTS chunks, lead = (t0 + window_start) − emit time, positive = early):

  | TTS delivery | batch 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | clamped |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | burst | +991 | +1191 | +1391 | … | | 0/8 |
  | 10× real time | −89 | +86 | +257 | +426 | +600 | 2/12 |
  | 2× real time | −345 | −245 | −156 | −80 | +18 | 6/12 |
  | 1× real time | −640 | −626 | −635 | −664 | −652 | 12/12 |
  | 10×, horizon 0.04 | −38 | +135 | +304 | | | 2/13 |
  | 10×, horizon 0.04, batch 100 ms | −20 | +151 | +323 | | | 2/14 |
  | 2×, horizon 0.04, batch 100 ms | −78 | +10 | +99 | | | 3/14 |

  The design's 200 ms lead is only met from the 3rd–4th batch even at 10× delivery. Providers stream per sentence in bursts that are typically 5–20× real time after their TTFB, so in practice **the first ~0.4–0.6 s of every turn is late**, and the client (`feed.ts:123–147`) anchors the whole utterance on that late arrival (+200 ms assumed lead), then "resyncs" with a visible jump when an on-time batch arrives. The same hold is the "tail latency during a mid-turn stall" follow-up in `pipecat-1.10-update.md`: the last ~0.6 s of keyframes before a gap wait for the next sentence's audio.
- Proposal: (a) split the horizon — gate keyframe windows on `cursor ≥ window_end` (keyframes are final at their hop once the centered median's one-hop buffer is accounted for; they have no retroactive dependency, verified), release events **per kind**: NASAL with the keyframes (it latches in 1–2 hops), CLOSURE candidates (≤ 250 ms dip + confirmation) and SILENCE (which is back-dated by 300 ms today: emitted 15 hops after its stamped start, `:688–698`) through a separate late-events path that attaches to whatever batch ships next, dropping any event whose `t0 + offset − lead < now` server-side (spec §1.3 "never late"); keep trailing-speech confirmation for closures (candidates mostly confirm within the hop that creates them; onset emission was measured at +31 % events, 19 % on real bilabials) and let confirmed closures ride late; stamp silence at detection time; use a 100 ms *first* window (fast start) then 200 ms. Measured through the real pipeline (first-batch lateness, ms): 1× 637 → 205 → 103 (split → split + 100 ms first window), 2× 347 → 113 → 56, 5× 151 → 49 → 24, 20× 57 → 20 → 9; lead ≥ 200 ms from window 0.4 s at 5× instead of 0.6 s; event sets identical. This also fixes the post-gap case (the first window after a playout gap is clamped 74 ms late today because post-gap audio must re-satisfy the 0.4 s gate). **Ship it with the client change in §5.1**: late-confirmed events ride in whichever batch is open with offsets earlier than the window, and `feed.ts:120–128` currently derives both the anchor and the "context restarted" test from `keyframes[0] ?? events[0]`, so an events-only late batch would blank the mouth; anchor and restart on `ws`/keyframes only. (b) `batch_window_ms` 200 → 100 as the documented mitigation for ~1× providers (message rate stays < 10/s, bytes/msg halves); (c) the idle flush of §4.2 for the stall case; (d) carry `ws` and the actual release `lead` on the wire so the client anchors on the truth instead of assuming +200 ms and clamped batches no longer poison the anchor (v2 wire change, §5.5); (e) the performance harness with a pacing sweep ([BENCH-8]) and a unit test that asserts lead ≥ 0 for batches ≥ 2 under 2× pacing.
- Impact: up to ~0.5 s less start-of-turn latency; removes the anchor jump; no composite change (offline benchmark), which is itself a benchmark gap (§6.9).

### 4.2 [LAT-2] Mid-turn stall holds the last ~0.6 s of keyframes (P1 bug, S)
- Evidence: `_analysis_task_handler` parks unconditionally on `await self._wake.wait()` (`:430–431`); only new audio or `TTSStoppedFrame` can release windows held at `:522`. Experiment: 1.0 s of audio at 10×, a 1.5 s stall, then 0.5 s + stop → windows [0.4, 0.6), [0.6, 0.8), [0.8, 1.0) were emitted 1256 / 1088 / 914 ms after their audio played. The client (`feed.ts:211–217`, 250 ms hold then ease) rests the mouth from ~0.65 s while audio plays to 1.0 s, and the late batches are behind the playhead and never rendered. With one TTS context per turn (pipecat ≥ 1.8) this happens at every LLM sentence gap.
- Proposal: before parking, compute the release deadline of the oldest held window (`t0 + window_start + shift − lead`, on `self.get_clock()`, the same clock as `t0`) and `wait_for(self._wake.wait(), timeout)`; on timeout call `_emit_batches(..., horizon=0.0)` to release every window with `window_end ≤ cursor`, including a partial window `[window_start, cursor)` (the next window starts at the cursor); debounce with an ingest-idle grace of ~80 ms (`deadline = max(playout deadline, last_audio_arrival + 80 ms)`) — a naive flush fragments the stall case into 11 batches, the debounced one gives 7 with the held window `[0.80, 0.98)` emitted exactly at its pts and no-stall runs unchanged; events keep their own horizon per [LAT-1]; re-check the generation counter after the timeout so an interruption during the wait discards the flush. Regression test: the stall scenario above (reproduced to the millisecond by an independent verifier), asserting no batch is emitted after its audio.

### 4.3 [PROC-1] A ≥ 2 s burst on the frame path silently drops audio and mis-places the SILENCE (P1 bug, S — this happens in production)
- Evidence: `:351–356` drop the oldest bytes when the 2 s ring overflows; `_merge_result` (`:491–501`) appends the SILENCE at `context.cursor` *after* merging the post-drop result. 6 s of audio queued without inter-frame sleeps → `bytes_dropped` = 128 000 B (4.0 s), one 2 s drain, first keyframe at 4.01 s, the only event a SILENCE at ~5.9–6.0 s (end of the analysed span) instead of at the start of the dropped span. Mechanism: `FrameProcessor`'s queue handler and `push_frame` into unbounded queues never yield while frames are queued, so a producer that enqueues > 2 s in one stretch starves the analysis task until the ring has already wrapped. This is not a test artefact: the stock **Google, AWS Polly, Inworld and MiniMax** TTS services chunk a complete HTTP response in a tight `for` loop, so with those providers *any sentence longer than 2 s* hits the drop path in production and the client shows a resting mouth until offset 4.0 s of the sentence.
- Proposal: drop only audio whose playout has already passed (`offset < now − t0 − shift − lead`) with a large hard cap (~30 s); place the SILENCE at the start of the dropped span with `duration` = dropped seconds; clear the analyzer's utterance state (partial frame, `prev_sample`, closure/silence run state) and the resampler across a drop; downmix/reject `num_channels != 1`; add the drop-path test. Raising the cap needs no analyzer buffer change (it already chunks into its 2 s scratch and runs 51–53× real time here). Land with [PERF-2] so bigger buffers do not mean bigger single drains. Run the processor tests on Python 3.12/3.13 in CI: `asyncio.wait_for(queue.get())` yields three times per call on 3.11 but zero times on 3.12/3.13, so the starvation profile differs from what the 3.11 venv shows.

### 4.4 [PERF-2] One drain blocks the shared event loop for up to 37 ms (P3, S)
`_process_context` (`:452–470`) drains the whole ring in one synchronous `analyze()` call (RTF 0.017–0.022 → 34–45 ms for 2 s). Every session in a dev-runner process shares one loop and SmallWebRTC emits one RTP packet per 10 ms from it, so a 37 ms stall delays 3–4 packets for every session. Feed the analyzer in ≤ 0.5 s slices with `await asyncio.sleep(0)` between them (generation check per slice), or `asyncio.to_thread`; plus the `np.median` replacement from §2.5. Add a loop-lag assertion to the processor tests.

### 4.5 Verified-correct and remaining test gaps
Correct: t0 anchoring at `max(now, playout end of queued audio)`, playout-gap shifts and the never-straddle rule, generation counter on interruption, reopened context ids, `transport_destination` propagation, monotonic `pts`, sample-count offsets. Untested: drop/`skip_offset` path; `_END_DRAIN_TIMEOUT_SECS`; `_MAX_CONTEXTS` eviction (an evicted unflushed context never calls `analyzer.flush`, and `_hops` is reset only in flush/reset/start, so the next context's offsets shift by the evicted utterance's length — reproduced: ~0.48 s shift; one-line fix: when the evicted context is the active one, schedule `analyzer.reset()` from the analysis task before the next `analyze`); delivery to a registered `MediaSender` destination; gap + reopen combinations; runtime settings edge cases (`dead_band`/`heartbeat_ms` set via `LipsyncUpdateSettingsFrame` are silent no-ops because they are baked into the analyzer at construction, `:216–219`, while the docstring says they apply — forward them to the analyzer at update time or refuse them with a warning, and test that every `LipsyncParams` field either takes effect or is refused).

### 4.6 [PROC-2] The stream resampler is never flushed at context close: the last 40–60 ms of every context is never analyzed (P2 bug, S)
Verified through pipecat's `create_stream_resampler`: 1.0 s of 24 kHz input in 20 ms chunks yields 15 400 of the expected 16 000 samples (37.5 ms held), 44.1 kHz 16.75 ms, 48 kHz 37.5 ms, 8 kHz 52.5 ms, 16 kHz 0; the tail is held, not lost, and a following silent chunk releases it. `_process_context` (`:457–459`) resamples per drain and `flush` never pushes a tail, so utterance-final sounds (the sentence-final stops of §3.10, the final vowel decay) produce no keyframes or events and the client holds the last pose for 250 ms then eases to rest. Proposal: when `context.closing` is set, push ~60 ms of zeros through the resampler before the final analyze/flush and let the analyzer discard the padded tail beyond `samples_seen` (or add a flush to pipecat's resampler upstream); test that a 1.0 s 24 kHz context yields the same hop count as a 16 kHz one.

### 4.7 Drop-in ergonomics (P2, L)
- Today: `server/pyproject.toml` has no build system, so `lipsync` is importable only from `server/`; users copy the folder and hand-place two processors around `transport.output()`; the client protocol/feed is not a package; the wire schema exists only as prose in `rtvi.py`.
- Proposal: publish **`pipecat-ai-lipsync`** (following pipecat's extension naming; module `pipecat_lipsync`, runtime dep numpy only) exposing `LipsyncProcessor`, `LipsyncMessageRelay`, `LipsyncParams` and a `lipsync_processors(params) -> (processor, relay)` helper; ship `schema/bot-tts-lipsync.v2.json` (JSON Schema, versioned); publish an npm `@pipecat-ai/lipsync-client` (or `pipecat-lipsync-client`) with the framework-agnostic feed, a `useLipsync` React hook and mapping tables to standard rigs; key adaptive state by an **app-supplied** voice id via `LipsyncUpdateSettingsFrame` (or a `voice_key` callable polled at `TTSStartedFrame`) — *not* by observing `TTSUpdateSettingsFrame`: the stock `TTSService` consumes that frame and never forwards it downstream (`tts_service.py:880–884`), so a processor placed after the TTS cannot see it ([ADAPT-2], after the persisted cache); make `dead_band`/`heartbeat_ms` live settings; document that the relay depends on the RTVI observer that `PipelineWorker` attaches by default (nothing states this today; `bot.py` relies on it silently, and the observer dedupes the re-pushed frame by id so the downstream pass through the assistant aggregator is safe); document the Daily (app-message) and websocket paths.
- Telephony: websocket output paces writes in real time, so `pts` release is real-time relative to send, but the client playout delay is unknown (the `lead` field of §5.5 is the fix); Twilio/Telnyx serializers write server-messages as raw JSON onto the media stream where no client renders them, so default `enabled=False` on phone legs (or a `destinations` filter on the relay, plus `dest` in the payload for multi-destination bots) and document the `ParallelPipeline` phone + avatar pattern.
- Upstream: an in-framework variant was explored and set aside (`pipecat-1.10-update.md`); the standalone package is the right unit until the wire format is v2-stable and a second client (Daily/Simli-style avatar) consumes it.

### 4.8 Text is already in the frame stream (P2, L — the tier after formants)
`process_frame` (`:280–296`) handles only TTSStarted/Audio/Stopped. Verified with a fake TTS service and a tap at the processor's position: in SENTENCE mode (the default) the per-sentence order is `AggregatedTextFrame(text, context_id, will_be_spoken=True)` → `TTSStartedFrame` → audio → `TTSStoppedFrame` → `TTSTextFrame` (`tts_service.py:1226–1244, 1325–1344`); in TOKEN mode the sentence anchor is emitted once a boundary is detected, possibly after some audio — and with a non-streaming service in TOKEN mode **no text frame reaches the processor at all**, so a text-aware tier must treat "no text for this context" as the normal fallback. `on_tts_request(tts, context_id, text)` also fires before synthesis. Providers with timestamps push per-word `TTSTextFrame`s with `pts = baseline + t` on the same clock and anchoring rule as our `t0`, so word onsets in context seconds are approximately `(frame.pts − t0)/1e9` — for 13 services including the quickstart's Cartesia default. Caveat (verified): the TTS service's word baseline (`_initial_word_timestamp`, set at its first audio) and our `t0` (first audio at the processor, or the queued-playout end) are different clocks-of-record, so estimate the per-context offset between them (first `TTSTextFrame.pts` vs the context's first-audio arrival) or use word pts as soft alignment priors; when contexts queue, prefer the audio-sample position (`samples_seen` when the `AggregatedTextFrame` arrives) as the sentence start. Azure's viseme events and ElevenLabs' character alignment are **not** wired in 1.10 (Azure subscribes only to word boundaries; ElevenLabs collapses characters to words), so "Tier 1 provider visemes" cannot be built without a fork or an app-level service subclass; word timestamps are what exists. None of the above needs a fork. See §8 stage D.

## 5. Client (`client/src`)

`LipsyncFeed` is the right foundation: DOM-free, a pure function of the wall clock over the keyframe buffer, frame-rate independent by construction. What limits it is the timing anchor and the unused event channel. ### 5.1 [CLIENT-1] The anchor is hundreds of ms late at every utterance start, then staircases (P1 bug, S — needs the wire change in §5.5)
- Evidence: `feed.ts:120–123` sets `impliedAnchor = now + 200 ms − first_offset` and `:137–141` only ever moves the anchor earlier. Because the server releases early batches late (§4.1) and clamps `pts`, the anchor is **+284 ms (10× TTS), +541 ms (2×), +836 ms (1×)** late at batch 1, then jumps forward in 100–200 ms steps as later batches arrive with more lead. Independently, anchoring on the first *keyframe* offset instead of the window start (`:120`; with dead-band/heartbeat gating the first keyframe of a window sits anywhere in [ws, ws + 0.2)) leaves the settled anchor **70–90 ms early** for the rest of the utterance (−70 ms at 10×, −69 at 2×, −89 after a 0.7 s mid-utterance pause), which the user currently trims away by eye and which changes per utterance.
- Proposal: carry `ws` (= `window_start + playout_offset`) and the actual release `lead` on the wire (§5.5), anchor on `now + lead − ws`, keep the min-filter for jitter only (bounded, ~40 ms, and allow a slow forward drift so one early outlier cannot pin the anchor; show `lead < 200 ms` in StatsBar as "content late by N ms"): simulated anchor error 0 ms at every batch, no jumps, at 10×, 2×, with pauses and with barge-in (two independent simulations agree: today +274/+542/+831 ms after batch 1 at 10×/2×/1× with staircase jumps of 40–215 ms; with `ws` + `lead` 0 ms). Compute `lead` in the relay against the transport's **real** start: the processor's `t0` is when the first chunk passed it, but the output transport buffers 4 × 10 ms chunks before its first write and pushes `BotStartedSpeakingFrame` at that write — measured 40–60 ms later at real-time pacing — and the relay sits after the transport and sees that frame, so it can record the per-context start and emit `lead = (t0 + ws + shift + (bot_started − t0)) − now`. Derive the "context restarted" test (`feed.ts:128`) from `ws`/keyframes only and treat `ev` entries with offsets earlier than `ws` as late confirmations that only append — required once [LAT-1] lets events arrive late. Interim without a protocol change: seed the first anchor from the SDK's `onBotStartedSpeaking`.

### 5.2 [CLIENT-2] Events never reach the rendered pose (P1, S)
`Mouth.tsx:88–95` / `WireframeMouth.tsx:266–273` use `activeEventKinds()` only for a badge; `sample()` (`feed.ts:179–218`) never consults `this.evs`. Vowel / 80 ms silent dip / vowel: the closure event fires (60 ms, conf 1.0) while the keyframes across the dip go 0.62 → 0.53 → 0.45 → 0.39 → 0.64; a −40 dB voiced-stop dip leaves them at 0.62–0.70. Proposal: apply events as overlays inside `sample()` — closure → a brief openness dip (≤ 20 ms attack, ~40 ms release over max(duration, 60 ms)), which is correct for every stop, with lips-pressed styling (width −0.1, rounding 0) only for closures carrying a bilabial label once the server can provide one. Event confidence *is* useful for the dip's depth — it tracks dip depth cleanly (1.00 / 0.94 / 0.80 / 0.37 for silent / −40 / −30 / −20 dB dips; full overlay ≥ 0.7, half at 0.4–0.7, none below) — but not for bilabial-ness (§3.2), so it must never decide lip-seal vs jaw-dip; nasal → honour `duration 0` as "until the next keyframe with openness > 0.2" (keyframes already carry the 0.05 cap); silence → ease to rest from the event offset with τ ≈ 120 ms instead of the 250 ms hold. The closure overlay is a **place-agnostic aperture close** (openness → ~0.05, width and rounding untouched) because the event stream is not bilabial-specific (9 events for 7 bilabials in "Bob put a big pepper…", 6 for 3 in harvard-01). Expose `closure` in `ArticulationSample` so rig mappers can drive `mouthClose`/`PP` directly.

### 5.3 [CLIENT-3] Barge-in is not handled: ~400 ms of stale motion, then hold + ease (P1 bug, S)
`App.tsx:31–38` registers only `onServerMessage`; `feed.reset()` runs only on manual Disconnect. On interruption the transport discards unplayed batches and the observer emits `bot-stopped-speaking` (and `bot-interrupted`, which client-js 1.13 does not yet surface); already-received future keyframes keep animating ~373 ms after the cut, then 250 ms hold + 300 ms ease. Proposal: `LipsyncFeed.cut(nowMs, {graceMs, fadeMs})` scoped to the current context, with two policies: on interruption (until client-js exposes `bot-interrupted`, treat a `bot-stopped-speaking` that arrives while keyframes still extend > 150 ms past the playhead as one) grace 50 ms + fade 100 ms; on a normal-end `bot-stopped-speaking` grace ≥ 150 ms so the jitter-buffered tail still plays. Measured stale motion after a 5× cut: +155 ms today, +15 ms with the correct anchor. Also cut on transport `disconnected`; an upstream client-js `onBotInterrupted` is worth a PR.

### 5.4 [CLIENT-4] Confidence → opacity contradicts the contract, but the contract needs a calibrated confidence first (P2, S)
`Mouth.tsx:83` / `WireframeMouth.tsx:174` render low-confidence frames at full excursion but translucent; the contract (`types.py:49`, spec §4.4) is `lerp(neutral, estimate, confidence)`. Implementing that contract today would **collapse articulation**: with a warm analyzer (c_conv = 1) on proxy speech 88–91 % of voiced hops score confidence < 0.3 and none > 0.8 (c_lpc ≈ 0.4 × c_fit ≈ 0.4 multiply; the plans record mean 0.04 cold on real fixtures), and `lerp(0.35, kf, conf^0.7)` shrinks speech-keyframe openness p90 from 0.82 → 0.49. Proposal: server prerequisite [CONF-1] (drop `c_snr`, recalibrate `c_fit`, normalize `c_lpc`/`c_fit` against a session running P90 so warm clean speech sits ≥ 0.8, verified by the harness's `conf_on_accurate − conf_on_inaccurate` gap); then client `shape = lerp(neutral, kf, 0.5 + 0.5·conf)` with `neutral = lerp(REST, SCHWA{0.35, 0.35, 0.1}, energy)` — never below half excursion — and remove the opacity coupling now regardless (keep it as a debug toggle at most).

**Interpolation: keep linear.** Measured against the server's dense per-hop conditioned track, the analyzer emits a keyframe at nearly every 20 ms hop during speech (69–84 keyframes over 2.7 s, median spacing 20 ms), so the 12.5 openness/s "velocity corners" are the server's slew clamp sampled faithfully; PCHIP/Catmull-Rom change MAE by < 0.001 (cosmetic C1 continuity, no overshoot), a σ = 20 ms Gaussian *doubles* the error, and any causal smoother (EMA τ 40 ms, critically damped springs) adds 10–47 ms of lag, is frame-rate dependent, and a semi-implicit spring diverges at 30 Hz without ≤ 4 ms substeps. Keep `sample()` stateless and linear (PCHIP if C1 is wanted), never causal; shorten `PREROLL` to ~80 ms only after the anchor fix (today the 200 ms pre-roll partly masks the late anchor); asymmetric timing belongs to the event overlays only. When a phone sequence exists (stage D) the *server* can blend targets with Cohen–Massaro dominance functions.

## 6. Benchmarks (`server/benchmarks`)

### 6.1 What 70.9 means
The harness is a sound DSP diagnostic (Praat sampled at our hop centres agrees with exact synthetic truth to 5–14 Hz on realistic five-resonator signals; the soxr ingest mirror gives the same formant error from 8 kHz to 48 kHz) but a weak score. By construction 30 of the 100 points are free: the closure/nasal/silence components (`accuracy.py:49–58`) score count ranges only (`corpus.yaml:37–49`), so bilabial-mama passes "4–14 closures" with **0.00 precision and 0.00 recall** at ±40 ms against the eight true /m/ onsets on the espeak proxy, and a fricative sentence with no bilabials produced 11 closures; `convergence_s` (`accuracy.py:170–190`) compares a clip to its own final state, so any clip under 3 s scores 100 even with ranges 35 % off the voice's converged range (measured: a 2.45 s clip that never settled → 100; an 11.9 s clip whose P² edges keep drifting → 0). The one non-free part of those 30 points is the nasal-hum `openness_p90_max` check, which sits in the nasal bucket (`CHECKS:72`). Roughly: 70.9 ≈ 29/40 on L1 (76/158 Hz), ~12/30 on L2 (r ≈ 0.33 each), ~25–30/30 free. The composite is also not comparable across `--sentences` subsets or clips with unmeasurable components: NaN components are dropped with weight renormalization (`:354–360`), so making `width_r` NaN *raises* the score 71.1 → 76.8.

**Staging.** A *v1.5* composite needs no new ground truth and can land in a day: drop `convergence_s` from the composite until [ADAPT-1] is fixed, then redefine it as time until cold ranges are within 10 % of the same voice's `--warm` final ranges; move the count checks to pass/fail guards outside the composite and keep `openness_p90_max` as a scored nasal guard; add `lag_ms` and CCC with NaN → 0; score L1 as median |ΔF1| plus gross-error rate plus coverage. The v2 composite with the L4 layer (§6.6) follows once phone truth exists. The L1 full-marks thresholds (50/80 Hz) sit at or below inter-tracker noise, so "90+" under this composite is not a meaningful target. Pearson r ignores gain (r = 1.00 on a 0.3 + 0.3·x track), jitter (r = 0.99 at σ 0.08) and, most importantly, **timing lag** — the largest perceived-sync defect a benchmark should catch (§6.3).

### 6.2 [BENCH-1] L1 rewards abstaining (P1, S) — do this before [DSP-1]
`accuracy.py:208–209` masks MAE to frames where `ours_f1 > 0`; the debug tap writes `f1 = 0.0` whenever the strict slot is empty (`formant_lipsync_analyzer.py:556–557`) although the mapped openness on that hop comes from a held/decayed value or `f1_broad`. Nothing reports how many Praat-voiced frames were scored: on the espeak proxy L1 scored only 20/68, 16/65, 15/77, 34/79 voiced hops per clip; on the real clip the baseline commits F1 on **0 %** of Praat-voiced hops and reads f1_mae = NaN. MAE is also outlier-dominated (bilabial-bob f1_mae 97 vs median 30). The decision log records the trap ("blanket admission broke the f1_mae guard", `accuracy-improvements-2.md`). Note that L2 (`:231`, masked on Praat voicing only) *can* see coverage gains — it is what moved the proxy composite 66.4 → 74.6 — while L1 hides them and f1_mae rises with coverage (20 → 26 → 36 Hz at 0.76 → 0.94 → 0.98). Add `l1_coverage` (scored / Praat-voiced hops) as a scored component (full ≥ 0.9, zero ≤ 0.5) — defined for every clip (0.0 when nothing commits), because `composite_score` (`:354–358`) silently drops NaN components and renormalizes the weights, so a zero-coverage clip vanishes from f1_mae today; treat sub-threshold coverage as zero L1 marks, not missing data, or charge uncovered Praat-voiced frames the zero-marks error (200/300 Hz); report per-clip committed-frame counts next to MAE; add `f1_used`/`f2_used` and a `held` flag to `LipsyncDebugFrame` and score the value that actually drives the mouth on **all** Praat-voiced hops; replace the ramp inputs with median absolute error plus a gross-error rate (|ΔF1| > 150 or |ΔF2| > 300 Hz) and keep MAE as a diagnostic. Expect the composite to drop on re-baseline and [DSP-1] to show its real gain.

### 6.3 [BENCH-2] Timing lag is invisible (P1, S)
`openness_r`/`width_r` are Pearson r at zero lag (`accuracy.py:234–241`). A perfect-but-lagged track scores r 0.99/0.93/0.87/0.75 at 20/40/60/100 ms; our own emitted track's best cross-correlation shift is +40 ms on five-resonator synthetic speech (r 0.905 → 0.940) and +40…+80 ms on the espeak proxy (r 0.37 → 0.52, 0.27 → 0.51, 0.42 → 0.58 when shifted); the source is the conditioning chain (§3.1). A second measurement puts the deterministic part at exactly one hop (+20 ms, conditioned-vs-raw, r 0.946 → 0.980) with the slew adding an amplitude-dependent remainder, and the espeak best lag at +40…+100 ms on 8/10 clips but at the search boundary on 2/10 — so compensate only the deterministic hop (§3.1; a fixed −40 ms over-corrects small swings by ~7 ms while −20 ms leaves +13/+32 ms on mid-crossings — measure on real fixtures whether a further 10–20 ms is warranted), attack the slew part by raising `_SLEW_MAX_PER_HOP` once jitter is scored, and report `lag_ms` = 20 ms × argmax over τ ∈ [−6, +6] hops of r(ours(t − τ), target(t)) on Praat-voiced hops (5 ms grid) **with a significance guard** (only when `r_best − r_0 ≥ 0.05` and |τ| is strictly inside the window; otherwise NaN), scored |lag_ms| (full ≤ 20 ms, zero ≥ 80 ms) primarily on the exact-truth synthetic tier where it is unambiguous. This is the metric that makes [COND-1] and [LAT-1] visible.

### 6.4 [BENCH-3] Shape statistics (P2, S)
Replace the r ramps with Lin's concordance correlation (CCC = 2·cov / (var_x + var_y + (μx − μy)²)) between the P10–P90-normalized reference (matching the analyzer) and our raw 0..1 track (no per-clip normalization of ours), computed on the post-convergence window with the full-clip value reported; it subsumes the p90 gain checks and exposes peak compression directly (a 0.3 + 0.3·x compressed track: r 1.000, CCC 0.51; +0.2 offset: r 0.994, CCC 0.69) while staying robust to jitter (σ 0.08: CCC 0.93); keep r as a diagnostic. Add `jitter` = mean |second difference| of the interpolated openness over speech hops (≤ 0.05) and keyframe rate (12–25/s) as **guards** (report, fail on breach) rather than weighted components. Derive the default ceiling from median Praat pitch (> 165 Hz → 5500, else 5000), which also fixes the `--voices` override, pending the per-voice selection of §6.5. Report Praat's own uncertainty per voice (fraction of voiced frames with < 3 formants or B1 > 400 Hz) and exclude those frames from L1 — logged as coverage so exclusion cannot become another abstain path. Set the L1 full-marks bar per voice pitch band from the measured Praat-vs-truth floor (Praat's own F1 error on clean synthetic vowels is ~33 Hz median at f0 300) rather than a global 50 Hz.

### 6.5 [BENCH-4] The Praat reference is ceiling-fragile (P2, M)
On the real clip |F1(5000) − F1(5500)| averages 205 Hz; at 5500 Praat's F1 bandwidth exceeds 500 Hz on 50 % of voiced frames (a merged pole); the same order-16 tracker scores f1_mae 10 Hz against the 5000 reference and 150 Hz against 5500. `--voices` hard-codes 5500 for every voice (`accuracy.py:481`), so a male voice run this way is referenced with the female ceiling. Select the ceiling **per voice**, offline, by minimizing the broad-root fraction (B1 > 400 or B2 > 600 Hz) over the voice's clips across 4500–6500 Hz in 250 Hz steps, or use Praat's per-frame ceiling optimisation (`To FormantPath (burg)` via `parselmouth.praat.call`); never a total-variance criterion (tried twice: it picks the *wrong* ceiling). On known-formant KlattGrid voices the truth-optimal ceilings were 5000–5250 (male, f0 100) and 6000–6250 (female, f0 200–240) — above the 5500 rule — and the reference's F2 is even more ceiling-sensitive than F1 (346 Hz error for a male voice at 4500). Replace the adjacent-ceiling mask with a plausibility mask on the reference (exclude F1 > 1100 Hz, F2 < F1 + 150 Hz, intensity > 30 dB below the clip max — low-energy voiced tails on the real clip read Praat F1 = 1606–1785 Hz with B1 up to 532 Hz and today pass every mask and distort the L2 normalization), report the excluded fraction as reference self-consistency, record the chosen ceiling, and fall back to a pitch-derived ceiling for ad-hoc voices. Praat with five expected formants is **not** a valid reference for the tests' three-resonator synth signals (F2 off by 645–725 Hz) — synthetic tiers must score against generator truth.

### 6.6 [BENCH-5] A phoneme-target layer ("L4") so consonants, closures, mapping and lag become measurable (P1, L)
- Why: L2 correlates against per-clip-normalized Praat F1/F2 over Praat-voiced frames (`accuracy.py:231–233`): it bakes in the 1-D mapping (any F3 or 2-D remap can only lower it), masks out every consonant, and events are counted not rendered. The decisions log rejected phoneme truth because "alignment needs MFA" (`benchmark-harness-accuracy.md:368`); that is no longer true.
- One schema: each fixture carries `phones.json` = `[{start_s, end_s, phone}]` (ARPAbet or IPA). Three tiers:
  - **Tier A — CI, no keys:** libespeak-ng via a 60-line ctypes shim (`plans/experiments/espeak_synth.py`) returns 22.05 kHz audio plus sample-accurate phoneme/word events; two independent prototypes in this review ran an 8-class viseme accuracy / closure P@40 ms / lag layer end to end on it. Scope it to what espeak can legitimately measure: closure P/R/F1 at ±40/60 ms, closure-on-other-stops count, nasal-closed fraction (as a regression only — see §0), `lag_ms` and keyframe economy; explicitly **no** openness/width MAE or viseme accuracy claims from espeak, which is formant-synthesized (our analyzer commits a strict F1 on only 10–44 % of its Praat-voiced hops at order 12, and holds a value on roughly half).
  - **Tier B — natural voices, keys once at fixture time:** Azure Speech SDK `viseme_received` (22 viseme ids, 100 ns offsets) as the one native-truth provider — which justifies adding Azure to the corpus — or Polly `SpeechMarkTypes=['viseme']`, written as sidecars at synthesis; Cartesia (and the other word-timestamp services) word timestamps captured for free in the existing headless synthesis mini-pipeline (`common.py`, extend `_AudioCollector` to record `TTSTextFrame.pts`) and combined with CMUdict for within-word placement — word-uniform phone placement measured 17 ms median / 75 ms p90 onset error on the proxy; or align the existing Cartesia/Deepgram fixtures with **pocketsphinx** 5.1.1 (29 MB wheel, bundled en-US model, BSD; `set_align_text` → `set_alignment` two-pass). Measured on the espeak proxy (out-of-domain audio) with a **normalized transcript** (lower-case, punctuation stripped — un-normalized text fails outright with "Unknown word"): 9/12 clips aligned, p/b/m onset error median 17 ms, 67 % within 40 ms, RTF 0.02; an earlier run without full normalization got 57 % within 40 ms with 10/64 failures. Gate each alignment on success and on word-span agreement with the provider word timestamps; fall back to word-level + CMUdict when the phone pass fails.
  - **Tier C — accurate one-off:** `ctc-forced-aligner` 1.0.2 runs on **onnxruntime only** (already a locked transitive dependency; torch appears only in its optional extras) with an ONNX model downloaded once and cached/committed, so no torch venv is needed — the first choice for authoring `phones.json` per fixture. Montreal Forced Aligner 3.4 (MIT; 21.9 ms mean phone-boundary error on TIMIT) is **not pip-installable in practice** — its Kaldi binding `kalpy` is not on PyPI — and charsiu is neither on PyPI nor usable without Hugging Face downloads, so both mean a separate conda environment, acceptable only for one-off fixture authoring; torchaudio `MMS_FA` aligns characters, not phones. Nothing heavy at re-score time; pocketsphinx stays the zero-setup option for word-level truth (the external survey rates it unreliable for phone truth; our measurement agrees it is marginal, and it failed outright on some espeak clips).
- Metrics (hop grid, per clip): phone → (openness, width, rounding, viseme class) via a canonical table with coarticulation-free plateaus; openness/width/rounding CCC over **all speech frames** and over vowels; per-class mean-shape table; 8-class viseme accuracy with a fixed decoder; closure precision/recall as **two numbers** — against any-stop onsets and against p/b/m onsets at ±40 ms (the detector fires on all stops by design, so bilabial precision reads ~0.1–0.15 until spectral place cues exist) — plus attribution of false closures to the phone class they landed on; `openness_p90 ≤ 0.15` during bilabial segments; nasal-closed fraction; `lag_ms`; keyframe economy. Baseline on the espeak proxy today: openness MAE 0.22, r(all speech) 0.31, r(vowels) 0.36; width r 0.36; rounding r 0.18; lag +1.2 hops; closure P/R 0.15/0.41 (192 of 416 events on t/d/k/g); bilabial openness 0.44; nasal closed 0.47; 30 kf/s; 8-class viseme accuracy 0.28.
- Composite v2 (100 pts, NaN scores 0 when the probe exists): L4 viseme accuracy 20 (full ≥ 0.80, zero ≤ 0.30); openness CCC vs jaw target 15 (≥ 0.80 / ≤ 0.20); width CCC 10; `lag_ms` 15 (≤ 20 / ≥ 80 ms); closure F1 at ±40 ms 15 (≥ 0.8 / ≤ 0.2); nasal guard 5; coverage-aware L1 (median error + gross-error rate + coverage) 10; keyframe economy/jitter 5; convergence vs the voice-level Praat P10/P90 5. ## 9. External context that shaped the ranking
- Every shipping real-time lipsync system that is not a vendor side-channel is amplitude/formant DSP (Live2D, VTube Studio, SALSA), MFCC template matching with per-voice calibration (uLipSync/wLipSync), or a very small sequence model on MFCC-class features (Meta OVRLipSync TCN; Adobe's 2019 real-time LSTM: 28-dim MFCC + energy at 100 Hz, < 200 ms latency, trained on 13–20 minutes of labels with time-warp augmentation). A numpy formant tracker with ~0.4 s of lookahead is squarely in the CPU-cheap regime, and the Tier 0.5 recipe in §8 is Adobe's, not wav2vec2's.
- Client rig standards: Oculus/OVR 15 visemes (`sil PP FF TH DD kk CH SS nn RR aa E ih oh ou`; Ready Player Me ships exactly these plus the 52 ARKit blendshapes; TalkingHead/HeadTTS consume `{visemes, times, durations}`), ARKit-52 (`jawOpen`, `mouthFunnel`, `mouthPucker`, `mouthClose`, `mouthPressL/R`; MediaPipe emits the same in-browser), Azure 22 ids + 55 blendshapes, Live2D 2 params, VRM 5 vowels. Continuous parameters cannot separate `DD/kk/nn/CH/SS`; collapse them to a consonant-neutral class and say so.
- Praat-class LPC trackers are ~2× human inter-labeller error and gender-biased (Praat defaults are tuned for female speakers); KARMA (Kalman ARMA over cepstra) beats Praat/WaveSurfer on F1/F2 mainly through temporal smoothing — which fits inside the batch hold. LPC formant error grows ~F0/4 (F1 error 41–58 Hz above 200 Hz; Vallabha & Tuller 2002), shared by the reference.
- Timing perception is asymmetric (ITU-R BT.1359-1: detectability +45 ms audio-lead vs −125 ms; acceptability +90/−190) and natural mouths lead the audio by 20–70 ms in CV syllables (100–300 ms utterance-initially): score mouth-lag > 45 ms as a miss but tolerate lead up to ~100 ms; do not pre-shift blindly (§3.1). The game-animation practice of critically damped springs was tested and refuted for this signal: τ 40–80 ms springs add 38–88 ms of lag and erase 42–64 % of closure dip depth; keep linear interpolation and measure smoothness and closure dip depth in the harness instead.
- Benchmark practice: per-frame viseme accuracy at 24–25 fps (Adobe, Meta) and phone-boundary P/R/F1 at ±20 ms plus R-value; SyncNet LSE-C/D and lip-vertex error need rendered video/meshes and do not apply here.
- Pipecat's avatar ecosystem (Simli, HeyGen, Tavus in core; Anam via `pipecat-anam`) renders server-side video from the TTS audio; position this feature as the client-rendered, any-TTS, bot-CPU alternative, keep the message shape adaptable to the Azure `{visemeId, audioOffset}` and TalkingHead conventions via the §5.7 mappers, and disable the processor when a video avatar service is downstream to avoid double lipsync.

## 10. Where reviewers disagreed, and what this document adopts
- **Closure rendering** — lip-press gated by event confidence (two client reviews) vs openness dip only (roadmap review): confidence is dip depth and reads 1.00 on alveolar/velar dips, so the server drives the openness target to ~0.05–0.1 during the low run, the event styles only, lip-press waits for the text tier.
- **Client smoothing** — look-ahead smoothing (confirmed lag-free) vs springs/EMA (literature) vs linear: keyframes are already near-dense in speech, so smoothing buys no accuracy, springs add 38–88 ms lag and erase closures; linear (PCHIP at most).
- **Keyframe time shift** — 20–60 ms anticipatory lead (analyzer, research) vs deterministic −20 ms only (benchmarks, roadmap): re-stamp exactly one hop, clamp ≥ 0; any perceptual lead is a client trim decided by the loopback rig.
- **Confidence formula** — drop `c_snr` vs gate on `c_snr·√c_conv` vs `c_fit` is the crushing term: the proxy and the real clip disagree 3× on `c_fit`, so calibrate on real fixtures against `conf_on_accurate/inaccurate`, fix `c_fit` first, gate `c_snr` only at the silence edge, and drop the client's opacity coupling now regardless.
- **Shift detector** — window-median variants (benchmarks) and both-edge tests (analyzer) both still false-fire; only pitch-median corroboration gave 0 false fires; explicit voice keying is the primary mechanism.
- **Voice keying** — observing `TTSUpdateSettingsFrame` (two reviews) cannot work (the TTS service consumes it); the voice id must come from the app.
- **Horizon split** — causal closure emission (three reviews) vs keep confirmation (+31 % events otherwise): keyframes + NASAL at `window_end` + 1 hop; CLOSURE and SILENCE as late events that only append; the client restart test keyed on `ws`.
- **Praat ceiling** — variance criterion (proposed) picks the wrong ceiling twice; pitch-derived default plus a per-voice bandwidth sweep and a plausibility mask.
- **LPC front end** — 10 kHz Praat-matched path (three reviews) vs order 16 at 16 kHz: equal accuracy on the proxy, 10 kHz is only the CPU variant; order 16 + NCC + slot prior first, 40 ms LPC window next.
- **Aligner tooling** — MFA (not pip-installable in practice), PocketSphinx (marginal but usable with a normalized transcript), ctc-forced-aligner (onnxruntime, needs one HF download), torchaudio: espeak exact tier for CI now; for real fixtures, provider word timestamps captured at synthesis plus one committed alignment authored offline, Azure visemes if Azure joins the corpus.
- **Nasal numbers on espeak** — "14 → 0 NASAL events at order 16" and "0 events in every configuration" are consistent only if the 14 were false positives on non-nasal sentences; neither says anything about real hums, which only the real nasal-hum fixtures can test.
- **CPU per hop** — quoted five ways (190 µs DSP primitives, 260–281 µs analyzer, 310–440 µs with debug tap/resampling, RTF 0.017): different scopes; use ~0.3 ms per 20 ms hop (~1.5 % of one core per speaking bot) as the reference and budget order 16 + NCC at +35–50 µs, offset by the −30 µs Levinson/eigvals wins.
- **Rounding** — lower gate + zero on nasal + F3 term vs evidence gate only: gate change alone, F3 fixture-gated, `rounding_p90(vowel-aa) ≤ 0.30` guard.
